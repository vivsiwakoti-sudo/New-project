Cambrian · shallow sea

A sea full of possibility

Photosynthetic microbes and algae fed tiny grazers, which in turn became prey for larger animals. Real food webs had many more links—and some animals, including Anomalocaris, likely ate larger prey too.

→  energy flows toward the eater
A planet in motion

Life shaped by the living world

Deep time was not a steady backdrop. Continents collided and split, sea levels rose and fell, and climate reshaped habitats. The Permian ended with Earth's most severe known mass extinction; in its wake, new communities arose in the Triassic.

One supercontinentPangaea assembled during the late Paleozoic and persisted into the Triassic.
Big climate swingsGlaciations, warming, and changing oceans shifted where life could thrive.
